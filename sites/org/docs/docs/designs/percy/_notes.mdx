I wanted a pair of shorts that had a fall front with a double row of buttons, a front pleat, and a flared
bottom edge gathered down into a cuff. So I made one!

If you live in a part of the world where "pants" means what I would refer to as "underwear," that's very
funny and also I'm not changing it. Feel free to giggle as much as you like when reading this.

The front construction is a simplified, not-especially-accurate rendition of the fall front breeches
common in the late 1700s and early 1800s. Most modern interpretations of this style have ornamental
buttons and an elastic waistband or a hidden side zipper, but I wanted these ones to be real.

This pattern can be short or long, flared or unflared, and cuffed or uncuffed, so you can produce
quite a wide variety of different pants. Only the fall front construction is non-optional.

Because of the way the front panel is constructed, the pockets are mandatory, and I made them
substantial - mine can easily fit a hardback book, a 700ml water bottle, or a can of pringles.
If you think you don't really need that much space, you can scale them down.

I have no idea how this pattern will behave if you have a belly. If that describes you and
you decide to make it, tell me how it goes! If there are any modifications you need to make for a
better fit, I'd be eager to add them to the design for everyone.
